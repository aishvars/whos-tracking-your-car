Right-to-Know request

Ask your local agency for its records about license plate readers. Fill in the boxes, copy the text, and follow the steps below. Everything stays in your browser: nothing you type is sent or saved.

The six requests ask for records, not answers, and avoid individual plate data and records about specific investigations, which the law lets agencies withhold.12

Your request

Before you send it

  1. Find the right officer. Every local and state agency must have an Open Records Officer. Look on the agency’s website, or search the Office of Open Records’ directory.11
  2. City of Pittsburgh. Requests go to the City’s Open Records Officer, 313 City-County Building, 414 Grant Street, Pittsburgh, PA 15219, openrecords@pittsburghpa.gov.15
  3. Use the form. Use the agency’s own request form if it has one; otherwise use the Office of Open Records’ standard form.11 The City uses the standard form and says it may deny a request that arrives without it.15 Paste the text above into the form’s description of the records requested.
  4. Give your name and a street address. The City says it refuses anonymous requests, treats an email without a proper name or street address as anonymous, and does not accept P.O. boxes.15 You do not need to say why you want the records.12
  5. Send it and keep a copy. You can send a request by email, fax, U.S. mail or in person, addressed to the Open Records Officer. Keep a copy: without one, an appeal can be dismissed.11
  6. Fees. Filing a request is free, but you may have to pay fees before you receive records.11
  7. The City’s response. The City responds within five business days, and meets that duty by mailing its response on the fifth business day.16
  8. Who can file. The law defines a requester as a legal resident of the United States.12

Then see what happens after you file, including deadlines and how to appeal.
