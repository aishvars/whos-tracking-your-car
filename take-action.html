What You Can Do

Four ways to act on license plate readers in your community, using public processes that exist today. Wherever we state a rule, a deadline or a procedure, a numbered link points to the official source.

1

Write To Your State Legislators

Your state representative and state senator sit in the Pennsylvania General Assembly. Its official lookup takes a street address, city and ZIP code and shows who represents that address; only Pennsylvania addresses can be searched.14

Our campaign is about state law, so these are the people to write to. Tell them what you want in a few lines.

2

Ask Your Local Agency For Its ALPR Records

The Right-to-Know Law presumes that agency records are public unless an exception applies, and the agency must show why a record is not available.10 A request goes in writing to the agency’s Open Records Officer11 and does not have to explain why you want the records.12

The agency has five business days to respond in writing.12 Our tool writes the request for you. It asks for policies, contracts, retention settings, sharing agreements, search rules and reports.

3

Speak At A Council Meeting

Under the Sunshine Act, the board or council of a political subdivision must give residents a reasonable opportunity to comment on matters before it at each advertised regular and special meeting, before it takes official action.13 Check your own city, borough or township website for its meeting schedule.

Questions you could ask (our suggestions, not legal requirements): Does the department use license plate readers? How long is the data kept? Who else can search it or receive it?

Pittsburgh meeting schedule and Speaker Signup

In Pittsburgh

  • Sign up with the online Speaker Signup form, or call the City Clerk at (412) 255-2138.19
  • Speakers get three minutes at Council’s regular and standing committee meetings and at public hearings.19
  • Registration for regular and standing committee meetings closes an hour before the meeting begins.19
  • At a public hearing, people who did not register may speak for one minute after the registered speakers have finished.20
  • Post agenda meetings are open to the public, but there is no public comment.20

What Happens After You File A Right-To-Know Request?

  1. The ResponseWithin five business days the agency must respond in writing: grant the request, deny it and cite the legal basis, or invoke an extension of up to 30 calendar days for certain reasons.10, 12
  2. More TimeIf the agency needs more time, it must tell you in writing, give the reason and a reasonable date for its response, and estimate any fees. If that date is more than 30 days past the five business days, the request is deemed denied unless you agree in writing to the later date.12
  3. No ResponseIf the agency does not respond in time, the request is deemed denied, and you have the right to appeal to the Office of Open Records.10
  4. AppealTo appeal, file in writing within 15 business days of the mailing date of the agency’s response, or of a deemed denial. Include a copy of your original request and of the denial; an appeal without a copy of the request is dismissed.10, 12
  5. DecisionThe appeals officer issues a final determination within 30 days of receiving the appeal, unless you agree to more time.12 Either side can appeal that decision to court within 30 calendar days.10
  6. FeesA standard black-and-white page costs up to 25 cents, an agency may require prepayment if fees are expected to exceed $100, and it cannot charge for the time spent redacting or for legal review.10 The City of Pittsburgh provides copies free when copying fees are $5 or less.17
  7. ExceptionsNot every record is public: the law lists 30 exceptions in Section 708.10 One covers records relating to or resulting in a criminal investigation.12 That is why our request asks for policies, contracts and reports rather than records about any particular investigation.
