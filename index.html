Who’s Tracking Your Car?

Automated license plate readers (ALPRs) are cameras that capture every license plate that comes into view, with the place, date and time.1 We think Pennsylvania should regulate them, not ban them. See where the cameras are, how the data moves, and what you can do.

    Why Now?

    A single scan seems harmless. Kept over time and shared, scans add up to a record of where a driver goes.1 Concern is growing, and the rules are being written right now.

    • Company claim 5,000+

      Flock says more than 5,000 law enforcement agencies use its technology. That is the company’s own figure, which we have not independently verified.21

    • News reports No. 1

      Allegheny County has more ALPRs than any other county in Pennsylvania, according to published counts based on Finding Flock’s crowdsourced data.22, 23

    • As of mid-August 2026 50+

      As of mid-August 2026, more than 50 agencies or communities had canceled, suspended or rejected a Flock contract, or deactivated cameras, since the start of 2026, according to the DeFlock tracker as reported by Stateline.24

    • Federal bill H.R. 10691

      In Congress, H.R. 10691, the “Ban Flock Act,” was introduced on October 1, 2026 and referred to House committees. It would bar federal use of license plate readers without explicit statutory authorization and withhold certain federal grants from state and local governments that do not prohibit their own agencies’ use of them.25

    • Pennsylvania 190–13

      Lawmakers from both parties had introduced at least nine bills by early September 2026.22 House Bill 2771 passed the House 190 to 13 on October 19, 2026 and was referred to a Senate committee on October 26, 2026.26 Pennsylvania is not among the states with a law specifically about ALPRs, according to Finding Flock.22

    Why Regulate, Not Ban?

    Police say the cameras help find stolen cars and missing people, while critics warn that they can map people’s daily lives with little oversight.23 Our proposal takes both seriously. It keeps real-time alerts for stolen and wanted vehicles, and it limits how long plate history is stored, how it can be searched and who it can be shared with.
